Three retainer deals came from TikTok. None of them from cold pitching into a void. All of them from a combination of inbound interest (brands reaching out after seeing the content) and a specific outreach approach that I'll give you in full in this guide.

## How Brand Deals Actually Happen

Most creators think brand deals work like this: you reach a follower threshold, brands start reaching out, you negotiate and sign.

The reality is more nuanced. Brands reach out based on content performance signals, not follower counts. A creator with 10K followers and a video that hit 5M views will get more brand outreach than a creator with 100K followers whose content averages 20K views.

What brands are looking for:
- **View velocity:** Videos that move fast in the first 24-48 hours
- **Engagement quality:** Comments that reference the product specifically (not just "great video")
- **Audience match:** Does the content audience overlap with the brand's target customer?
- **Production quality:** Does the content look professional enough to represent the brand?

The AI skeleton format helps with all four of these. High completion rates drive view velocity. Product-focused content generates product-specific engagement. The format's visual quality looks professional. If your content is performing, inbound interest will come.

## Making Your Account Visible to Brands

Before outreach, your account needs to signal that you're a serious creator.

**Creator marketplace profile:** Complete your TikTok Creator Marketplace profile in full. Brands and agencies search the marketplace before reaching out directly. An incomplete profile is invisible. Your profile should include: niche description, audience demographics, average view count, and contact email.

**Business email in bio:** A business email address (not Gmail) signals professionalism. yourname@yourniche.com is fine. Get one before outreach begins.

**Content portfolio:** Pin three videos that represent your best work : high view count, high engagement, strong production quality. When a brand clicks your profile, these are what they see.

## When Inbound Interest Arrives

A brand reaching out is not a signed deal. Most inbound messages are exploratory : they want to know rates, exclusivity terms, and whether you can deliver what they need. Your response to that first message determines whether it becomes a deal.

**The first response:**
Don't immediately send a rate card. Instead, ask two questions:
1. "What does a successful campaign look like for you?"
2. "What's your timeline?"

This does two things: it shows you're thinking about their goals, not just your payment, and it gives you the information you need to price correctly.

**What "retainer" means in practice:**
A retainer deal is an ongoing agreement: X videos for X months, for a fixed fee. Both numbers should be agreed upfront and written down, not left as "we'll figure it out as we go." The advantage over one-off deals is predictable income. The disadvantage is commitment - you're locked into the brand's products and timeline for the length of the agreement.

When you're just starting out, a typical first offer looks like $200-300/month for 20-25 videos. That's a low rate per video, and brands will often try to keep you at that rate indefinitely if you don't push back. Treat it as a foot in the door, not a ceiling. As your views, sales, and credibility with that brand grow, you'll start getting pitched better deals - higher monthly fees, fewer videos required, or added perks like affiliate commission on top of the flat fee.

Before agreeing to a retainer, confirm the brand's product is one you can produce content for consistently. A product with limited visual angles becomes very difficult to make different videos for month after month.

## The Outreach Message (When You're Pitching)

This is the template I used for outreach that resulted in responses. Adapt it to your specific situation.

---

Subject: TikTok content partnership : [Brand Name]

Hi [Name],

I've been creating TikTok Shop content in the [niche] space for [time period]. My recent videos in this category have averaged [view count], with a [percentage]% completion rate and [CTR]% product click rate.

I think [Brand Name]'s [product] is a strong fit for my audience and the AI skeleton video format I use : [one sentence on why it fits visually or audience-wise].

I'd be interested in exploring a monthly content agreement. I typically produce [X] videos per month for brand partners and can share full analytics access so you can track performance directly.

Are you open to a quick call this week?

[Your name]
[TikTok handle]
[Email]

---

What makes this work:
- It leads with your performance data, not your follower count
- It references why their specific product is a fit (shows you've done research)
- It proposes a retainer (monthly) rather than a one-off, which signals long-term thinking
- It offers analytics transparency, which reduces the brand's risk

## Pricing the Retainer

Pricing is the question creators are most reluctant to ask other creators about. Here's a framework.

**Starting point for monthly retainer pricing:**
(Average views per video / 1,000) × $15 = base rate per video

Example: 500K average views = (500,000 / 1,000) × $15 = $7,500 per video

That seems high and it is : very few creators start there. The formula assumes premium CPM and negotiation room. In practice, expect to land at 30-50% of the formula rate when starting out, moving toward full rate as you build track record with brand partners.

For your first retainer deal, accept less than you'd prefer in exchange for a case study you can reference in future pitches. A verifiable "I ran a 3-month campaign for [Brand] that generated X results" is worth more in future negotiations than holding out for a higher rate on deal number one.

**What to include in the retainer:**
- Number of videos per month (specify)
- Exclusivity scope (are you blocked from promoting competitors? For how long?)
- Approval process (how many revision rounds before final delivery?)
- Analytics reporting (what data do you share and when?)
- Payment terms (net 15 or net 30, paid monthly in advance)

Get all of this in writing. A Stripe invoice and an email exchange confirming terms is sufficient to start. Formal contracts become important at higher deal values - anything under $2K/month, an email agreement is fine.

## If the Offer Isn't Right, Negotiate

Most creators accept the first number a brand offers because asking for more feels like it might kill the deal. In practice, brands expect a counter. The initial offer is rarely their ceiling, especially once you have any track record at all.

You don't need to accept or walk away as your only two options. A counter-offer that explains your reasoning, backed by your actual numbers, is normal and usually well received. Brands would rather negotiate with a creator who knows their value than start over with someone new.

**When to counter:**
- The rate is well below what your view counts would justify (use the formula above as your reference point)
- The video count is high relative to the pay (20-25 videos/month for $200-300 leaves very little per video)
- The exclusivity terms are broad relative to the pay (e.g., blocked from an entire category for a low monthly fee)

**Counter-offer template:**

---

Hi [Name],

Thanks for sending this over - I'm interested in working together. Before we lock in the details, I wanted to flag a couple of things based on how this content has been performing.

My recent videos in [niche] have averaged [view count] with a [percentage]% completion rate. At [X] videos/month, [proposed rate] works out to [$ per video], which is below where my rate normally lands for this volume.

Would [counter rate] for the same [X] videos/month work on your end? I'm also open to a structure like [fewer videos for the same rate] or [adding an affiliate commission on top of the base fee] if that's easier to fit into your budget.

Happy to hop on a quick call if that's easier to sort out.

[Your name]

---

If the brand can't move on rate, the alternatives in the template (fewer videos, added commission, shorter commitment) often get accepted even when the dollar figure can't. And if they decline all of it and the deal still doesn't make sense for you, it's fine to pass - a bad retainer locks up your content slots for low pay, which has a real opportunity cost against better deals coming later.

## What Brands Actually Want to Know

When a brand is evaluating you, the questions they're really asking are:

1. Can this creator deliver consistently, not just on one video?
2. Will this creator's audience trust a recommendation?
3. Is the content quality good enough to represent our brand?
4. What's the actual reach, not just the follower count?

Address all four in your pitch materials. Your case study (that first retainer deal at lower rates) directly answers question 1. High engagement rates answer question 2. The quality of your pinned videos answers question 3. Your view analytics answer question 4.
