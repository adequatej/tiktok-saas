Every TikTok video is a negotiation. In the first 3 seconds, you're asking a stranger to keep watching. They didn't follow you, they didn't search for you, they were in the middle of doing something else. You interrupted them.

If your opening line doesn't win that negotiation instantly, they're gone. Not "they might leave" — they are gone. TikTok's feed is frictionless in one direction: forward. And once someone scrolls, they don't come back.

This is the thing most creators understand in theory but don't apply in practice. They spend 80% of their production effort on the middle and end of their videos — the demo, the reveal, the call to action — and treat the hook as an afterthought. Then they wonder why their retention drops off in the first three seconds and the algorithm buries the video.

## Why 3 Seconds Is the Real Metric

TikTok's algorithm uses a metric called 3-second video views — the number of people who watched past the opening before scrolling. It's one of the primary signals used to decide whether to push your video to a wider audience or quietly stop showing it.

A video with a strong hook scores high on this metric and gets shown to more people. A video with a weak hook scores low and dies in the feed. The content after second 3 is irrelevant if nobody stayed to see it.

This means the hook isn't just the start of your video — it's the gate that your entire video has to pass through before anyone sees the product, the demo, or the CTA.

## What a Weak Hook Looks Like

The most common weak hook pattern I see from TikTok Shop creators: leading with the product.

"This [product name] is amazing and here's why..."

"I've been using [product] for two weeks and..."

"Check out this [product] I found..."

All of these put the product first and the viewer's interest second. The viewer doesn't care about the product yet — they care about themselves, their problems, and their time. Opening with the product tells them this video is about you trying to sell them something, which is exactly the signal that triggers the scroll reflex.

A strong hook opens with something the viewer is already thinking about — a problem they have, a result they want, or a pattern they recognize. The product is the answer to that problem, not the starting point.

## The Pattern Interrupt

Beyond leading with the viewer's perspective, the other function of a strong hook is the pattern interrupt: something that stops the automatic scroll before the conscious brain has made a decision.

This is why certain visual hooks (unexpected movement, surprising text on screen, an unusual opening frame) work independently of the audio. The eye catches something that doesn't match the expected feed pattern, and the brain pauses. That pause is your window.

Strong hooks do both: interrupt the pattern *and* speak to something the viewer already cares about. Either one alone is weaker than both together.
