Most creators look at view counts and move on. That's the equivalent of reading one number on a blood panel and calling yourself healthy. Views tell you almost nothing in isolation. The specific combination of metrics tells you exactly what to fix, what to repeat, and what to cut.

This guide covers the metrics that actually matter, how to interpret them in combination, and the decision framework I use after every video.

## The Metrics That Matter

### 3-Second View Rate

**What it measures:** The percentage of people who watched past the 3-second mark.
**Where to find it:** TikTok Studio > Video Analytics > Audience Retention

**Benchmark:** Above 40% is strong. Below 25% means the hook failed.

This is the first metric to check after a video posts. If it's below 25%, the algorithm has already decided the video isn't worth pushing. The content after second 3 is largely irrelevant. The fix is the hook : not the production, not the product, not the CTA.

### Completion Rate (Full Video Watch Rate)

**What it measures:** The percentage of viewers who watched to the end.
**Where to find it:** TikTok Studio > Video Analytics > Audience Retention

**Benchmark:** Above 30% is strong for videos over 30 seconds. Above 50% is strong for videos under 15 seconds.

Completion rate is your most direct measure of content quality after the hook. If 3-second rate is high but completion rate is low, your hook worked but the video failed to deliver on its promise. The fix is the bridge and pacing : Guide 03.

### Average Watch Time

**What it measures:** Average seconds watched per view.
**Where to find it:** TikTok Studio > Video Analytics

**How to use it:** Compare average watch time to total video length. If your video is 35 seconds and average watch time is 12 seconds, viewers are dropping at roughly the 1/3 mark. That's the specific section to fix in your next edit.

### Product Click Rate (CTR)

**What it measures:** The percentage of viewers who clicked your TikTok Shop product link.
**Where to find it:** TikTok Shop Studio > Creator Analytics > Video Commerce Data

**Benchmark:** Above 2% is strong. Above 4% is excellent. Below 1% suggests a CTA or product positioning problem.

CTR is where most creators' analysis stops. It shouldn't be : CTR tells you whether the video converted, not whether the purchase converted.

### Purchase Conversion Rate

**What it measures:** Of the people who clicked the product link, what percentage purchased?
**Where to find it:** TikTok Shop Studio > Order Data

**Benchmark:** 3-8% is typical for TikTok Shop. Below 3% points to a listing problem (not a video problem). Above 8% means your video is doing exceptional pre-selling work.

### Revenue Per View

**What it measures:** Total commission earned / total views.
**Calculate it manually:** Commission earned (from Shop Studio) / View count (from TikTok Studio)

This is the number that lets you compare videos across different view counts and products. A video with 2M views that generated $800 in commissions has a lower revenue-per-view than a video with 400K views that generated $300 in commissions. The second video's format and product combination is more efficient.

## Reading the Metrics in Combination

The power of these metrics is in how they interact. Here are the diagnostic combinations:

**High views, low 3-second rate, low sales:**
The algorithm pushed the video (suggesting the format has some signal) but the hook failed. Viewers left before the product appeared. Fix the hook. The product and production are probably fine.

**High 3-second rate, low completion rate, low sales:**
The hook worked. The video lost viewers in the middle. Find the drop-off point using the audience retention graph and fix that specific section.

**High completion rate, low CTR:**
The video held attention all the way through but didn't convert. The CTA failed or the product link was hard to find. Fix the CTA and confirm the product link is visible during the CTA. Also consider whether the product itself is the right fit for the audience.

**High CTR, low purchase conversion:**
The video sold the viewer. The listing failed them. Evaluate the product listing : images, reviews, price, description. Switch products if the listing can't be improved.

**High everything, moderate views:**
This is a distribution problem, not a content problem. The algorithm hasn't reached this video's full potential audience yet. Options: post at a higher-engagement time, add relevant hashtags, or boost with TikTok's promote feature.

## The Post-Video Decision Framework

After every video, run through this in order:

1. **Check 3-second rate within 2 hours of posting.** If below 25%, the video won't get algorithm push. Make a note of what the hook was and why it may have failed. Don't delete : let it sit.

2. **Check completion rate at 24 hours.** This is when you have enough data to make decisions. Note where the retention graph drops.

3. **Check CTR and purchase conversion at 48 hours.** By this point the initial distribution burst is done and the numbers are settling.

4. **Categorize the video:** Did it fail at Hook, Content, CTA, or Listing? This tells you what to improve next.

5. **Document the product.** Note the product, commission rate, purchase conversion rate, and whether you'd film it again.

## Doubling Down on What Works

When a video performs significantly above your average (3x or more views than typical), the instinct is to celebrate and move on. The correct move is to immediately analyze and replicate.

Within 24 hours of a breakout video, document:
- The exact hook (word for word)
- The product shown
- The opening visual frame
- The video length
- The posting time
- The audio track used

Then create 2-3 videos that deliberately replicate the key variables while changing one element at a time. This is A/B testing at TikTok's production speed. The goal is to identify which specific element of the breakout video drove the performance, not just to capitalize on luck.

If the second and third videos also perform, you've found a repeatable format. Systematize it and produce it consistently until the performance decays.

## When to Cut a Product

Stop filming a product when:
- Purchase conversion is below 2% after 5+ videos
- The product accumulates negative reviews that you can't address in your content
- Commission rates drop below your minimum threshold
- The niche becomes saturated enough that new videos are seeing declining view counts

Cutting a product is not failure. Continuing to produce content for a product that won't convert is failure. The five-filter system from Guide 07 is designed to minimize the number of products you commit to that end up in the cut category.

## Building a Simple Tracking Sheet

You don't need software. A spreadsheet with these columns is sufficient:

| Video date | Product | Views | 3s rate | Completion | CTR | Purchases | Commission | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Fill it in for every video within 48 hours of posting. After 10 videos, patterns emerge. After 20, you have enough data to make confident decisions about what to scale and what to stop.

The analytics system and the production system are designed to work together. Production gives you the content. Analytics tells you what the content is doing. Iteration closes the loop. Creators who only optimize production get incrementally better. Creators who run the full loop : produce, measure, iterate : compound.
