The fastest way to waste time as a TikTok Shop creator is to pick a product that can't succeed on the platform — and then wonder why your videos aren't converting.

I've done it. Most creators do it at some point. The issue is that TikTok Shop product selection looks straightforward on the surface: find something popular, make a video, collect commissions. But "popular" and "TikTok-viable" are not the same thing, and confusing them is expensive.

## The Most Common Mistake

New TikTok Shop creators default to one strategy: copy what's already working. They scroll the creator marketplace, find products with high commission rates or high sales numbers, and assume those signals mean the product is a good pick.

Sometimes it is. More often, those products are saturated — dozens of creators already have videos ranking for the same search terms, the audience has seen every angle, and you're entering a competition you can't win with a new account.

The other failure mode is picking based on personal interest. "I love skincare, so I'll promote skincare." Your interest in the category tells you nothing about whether the product films well, converts well, or has room for a new creator to build traction.

Product selection is a filter problem, not a discovery problem. You're not looking for good products in general. You're looking for products that are specifically good for *your format, your stage of growth, and TikTok Shop's conversion mechanics.*

## The Two Filters That Matter Most

Out of every criterion I run products through before filming, two eliminate the wrong choices fastest.

**Filter 1: Visual payoff potential.**

TikTok is a visual platform. The product either demonstrates its value visually — you can see it working, transforming, or producing a result — or it doesn't. Products that require explanation convert poorly on TikTok. Products that produce a visible result in under 15 seconds convert well.

Ask the question: *if I muted this video, would someone still understand why this product is interesting?* If the answer is no, the product is working against the format, not with it.

**Filter 2: Impulse price range.**

TikTok Shop buyers make decisions fast. They're in the feed, not in shopping mode. The products that convert consistently sit in a price range where the decision is easy — where the gap between "I'm curious" and "I bought it" is small.

Above a certain price point, people pause. They leave the app to research. They come back later (or they don't). Below a certain price point, the commission doesn't justify the production effort.

The right range isn't the same for every creator or every niche. But it exists, and once you know where your audience's impulse threshold is, you stop picking products that fall outside it.

## What These Two Filters Don't Tell You

Passing both filters means the product is worth considering. It doesn't mean the product is worth filming.

There are three more criteria I use before committing — covering margin relative to production cost, search demand signals, and competitive saturation by format type. Those filters are the difference between a product that generates a few hundred views and one that generates millions.

They're covered in full in the Founders Bundle under the Product Selection Playbook, including worked examples from products I've filmed and the decisions that led to them.

## The Practical Upshot

Before you pick your next product, run it through the two filters in this guide. Eliminate anything that fails either one.

You'll reject a lot of products you would have otherwise tried. That's the point. Every video you don't make on a bad product is time you can spend on a good one.

The creators who build consistent TikTok Shop income aren't making more videos — they're making better selections before they film anything.
