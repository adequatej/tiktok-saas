Every script I've written for a top-performing video follows the same three-part structure. Not because I decided on a format and stuck to it : because the structure is determined by how TikTok users process information in the feed.

Hook. Bridge. CTA.

Three parts. That's the whole framework. The skill is in executing each one correctly.

## Part 1: The Hook (0-3 seconds)

The hook's only job is to stop the scroll and earn 3 more seconds of attention. It does not introduce the product. It does not say your name. It does not explain what the video is about.

It does one of three things:

**1. States a result the viewer wants.**
"I made $5K last week selling one product from my phone."
"I went from 0 to 10M views in 30 days."

The viewer hears a result they want for themselves. They stay to find out how.

**2. Poses a question the viewer already has.**
"Why do some TikTok Shop creators make thousands per week while most make nothing?"
"Did you know this product exists?"

The question creates an open loop in the viewer's brain. Humans are wired to seek closure on open loops. They watch to get the answer.

**3. Makes a claim that sounds unlikely but credible.**
"This $12 product got me 3 brand deals."
"I almost didn't post this video."

The tension between "unlikely" and "credible" creates curiosity. They watch to see if it holds up.

### Hook Templates by Category

**Result-based hooks:**
- "I made [dollar amount] in [time period] from [specific action]."
- "This one change took me from [before state] to [after state]."
- "[Impressive result] from [simple or surprising input]."

**Question-based hooks:**
- "Why does [phenomenon] happen and most people don't know?"
- "What would you do if [desirable scenario]?"
- "Have you ever noticed that [observation]?"

**Claim-based hooks:**
- "I almost didn't share this because [honest reason]."
- "This is the [product] I've been keeping to myself."
- "Nobody talks about [product/method] but it [impressive result]."

### What to Avoid in the Hook

Do not open with: "Hey guys," "So today," "I want to show you," or any version of "Check this out." These phrases signal that the content is being presented at the viewer rather than created for them. The scroll reflex triggers.

Do not open with the product name. The viewer has no context for why they should care about your product yet.

## Part 2: The Bridge (3-30 seconds)

The bridge does the actual selling work. It has three jobs:

1. Establish the problem or desire in terms the viewer identifies with
2. Introduce the product as the solution
3. Demonstrate the value visually (this is where your AI video clips carry the load)

The bridge should move faster than feels comfortable when you're writing it. TikTok pacing is compressed. If a sentence doesn't add information or advance the story, cut it.

### Bridge Structure

**Step 1 : Acknowledge the viewer's situation (2-4 seconds):**
"Most people trying to make money on TikTok are posting the same type of content and wondering why it isn't working."

**Step 2 : Introduce the reframe or insight (4-6 seconds):**
"The ones making consistent income found one format that the algorithm keeps pushing : and it's not what you'd expect."

**Step 3 : Introduce the product as the vehicle (4-6 seconds):**
"This [product] is what I used to [result]. Here's exactly how it works."

**Step 4 : Demonstrate (remaining bridge time, 10-15 seconds):**
This is where your AI video runs. The script goes silent or shifts to a voiceover describing what's visible on screen. Let the visual do the work.

### Bridge Voiceover

Voiceover during the demonstration should be descriptive, not salesy. Describe what the viewer is seeing in terms of its benefit, not its feature.

Feature-driven (weak): "This serum has 10% niacinamide and hyaluronic acid."
Benefit-driven (strong): "This is what it looks like after two weeks : the difference is hard to miss."

Show, then describe the result. Don't describe the feature and ask the viewer to imagine the result.

## Part 3: The CTA (30-45 seconds)

The CTA has one job: convert. It should be direct, time-limited if possible, and make the next action obvious.

### CTA Templates

**Direct CTA:**
"Link is in my bio. It's [price] and ships fast."

**Urgency CTA:**
"This is selling out : link's below if you want it at this price."

**Social proof CTA:**
"I've ordered this three times. Link in my TikTok Shop below."

**Soft CTA (for awareness-stage content):**
"Follow for more : I post the stuff that actually works."

### CTA Placement

The CTA should begin exactly when the product demonstration ends. There should be no dead air between the end of the demonstration and the start of the CTA. Dead air is where the viewer's finger hovers over the scroll.

The TikTok Shop link should be visible on screen during the CTA. A text overlay reading "Link Below" or "Shop Now" should appear at the same time the CTA is spoken.

## Writing the Full Script

A 35-second script at natural speaking pace is approximately 90-100 words. That's it. Write tight.

Template:

```
[Hook : 10-15 words]

[Bridge setup : 20-30 words]

[Product introduction : 15-20 words]

[Demonstration voiceover : 15-20 words]

[CTA : 10-15 words]
```

Total: approximately 80-95 words for a 35-second video.

Write your first draft, read it aloud while timing it, then cut to hit the duration. Every cut makes the script stronger.

## Adapting Templates to Your Product

The templates above are structures, not word-for-word scripts. Fill them in with specifics:

1. Replace [result] with your actual result. Specificity converts better than generality. "$5K" converts better than "a lot of money."
2. Replace [product] with what you're actually selling.
3. Replace [time period] with the real timeframe.

The more specific the script, the more credible it sounds. Credibility is what earns the click.
