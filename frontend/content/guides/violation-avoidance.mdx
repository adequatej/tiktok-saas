Account restrictions and content strikes are the most underestimated risk in TikTok Shop. A single violation can freeze your Shop affiliate access, suppress your content in the feed, or in serious cases terminate your account entirely. This guide covers how to avoid them and how to recover when one happens anyway.

## How TikTok's Content Moderation Works

TikTok uses a layered system: automated scanning runs first, human review runs second for flagged content, and appeals go to a separate review team. Understanding this matters because it tells you how to handle each stage differently.

**Automated scanning** catches keyword matches, visual pattern matches, and link violations. It's fast and imprecise. A significant number of violations at this stage are false positives.

**Human review** applies to content that automated scanning flags as borderline. Human reviewers follow a policy checklist, not personal judgment. Content that's borderline under the checklist fails review. Content that's clearly fine passes quickly.

**Appeals** go to a different team than the original reviewers. An appeal is not a re-review of the same decision : it's a fresh evaluation by a different set of eyes applying the same checklist. This is why appeal framing matters.

## What Triggers Automated Flags

### Words and Phrases

The following types of language trigger automated flags when used in captions, text overlays, or voiceover:

**Health and body claims:** Any claim that a product treats, cures, or prevents a medical condition. This includes implied claims. "Helps with joint pain" triggers a flag the same way "cures arthritis" does. If your product has a health adjacent benefit, describe the experience, not the medical outcome. "Feels noticeably better after two weeks" is fine. "Relieves joint pain" is flagged.

**Income claims:** Specific income amounts in captions can trigger commercial content flags if not properly disclosed. If you include income figures in your caption, pair them with a disclosure ("results not typical" or equivalent). Income claims in voiceover over video content are generally not flagged by automated scanning : the flag risk is in the caption text.

**Superlatives and comparisons:** "The best," "better than," "#1" require substantiation under advertising guidelines. Use them sparingly in captions. They are lower risk in voiceover.

**Prohibited product categories:** Certain product categories cannot be promoted on TikTok Shop regardless of how they're framed. Supplements making specific health claims, products with restricted ingredients, and certain financial products are common categories. Check the TikTok Shop Prohibited Products list before committing to a product.

### Visual Patterns

**Before/after imagery:** Split-screen before/after comparisons are flagged by visual scanning, particularly for health, beauty, and weight-related products. Use sequential clips rather than side-by-side comparisons.

**Text on imported images:** If you import an image with text already on it (a product label, a brand graphic, a screenshot), automated scanning reads that text and applies its filters to it. This can trigger flags for text you didn't write and didn't intend.

**Third-party logos:** Showing a third-party brand logo prominently in your video without authorization can trigger an intellectual property flag. This includes visible brand names on clothing, products in frame, or in the background.

### Link and Disclosure Issues

**Undisclosed affiliate links:** TikTok Shop affiliate content requires a disclosure. The platform typically handles this automatically when you add a Shop link, but if you're linking to external products through other mechanisms, you need to add your own disclosure.

**Links in comments:** Adding affiliate links in comment replies can trigger spam flags, especially if the same link appears across multiple comments.

## Shadowban Patterns

A shadowban is not an official TikTok policy : it's the informal name for reduced distribution that doesn't result in a formal strike or notification. Signs of shadowban:

- Videos getting significantly fewer views than your recent average
- Your content not appearing in search results for relevant hashtags
- Followers reporting they're not seeing your content in their feed

Common triggers for distribution reduction:

- Posting the same video multiple times (even with minor edits)
- High skip rate in the first 3 seconds across recent videos (algorithm interprets this as poor content)
- Sudden posting frequency increase (posting 10 videos in one day after typically posting 1)
- Engagement bait in captions ("comment X if you want this")

Distribution reduction from behavioral patterns typically resolves itself within 2-7 days of returning to normal posting behavior. Unlike formal violations, there's no appeal process.

## The Appeal Process

When a video receives a violation strike, you have the option to appeal. Most creators write a generic appeal and get rejected. Here's what actually works.

**Step 1: Read the specific violation cited.**
TikTok tells you which policy was violated. Read the exact policy language, not your interpretation of it. Your appeal needs to address the specific language of the policy, not your general sense of fairness.

**Step 2: Write a factual appeal, not an emotional one.**
Appeals are reviewed against a checklist. "I didn't mean to violate any rules" doesn't help a reviewer check a box. "This content does not make a medical claim : it describes a personal experience with a product" gives the reviewer language to work with.

**Step 3: Point to specific content elements that comply.**
"The caption includes the required affiliate disclosure. The health language in the voiceover describes experience rather than medical outcome." Specificity helps the reviewer confirm your argument against the policy checklist.

**Step 4: Submit through Creator Center, not the in-app flag.**
The Creator Center appeal form gives more space for explanation than the in-app flag button. Use it.

**Step 5: If the first appeal is rejected, appeal again.**
A rejected first appeal can be appealed a second time. The second appeal goes to a different reviewer. Second appeals have a meaningfully higher success rate than first appeals because the second reviewer is evaluating fresh.

## Account Recovery After Serious Violations

If your account is restricted or suspended rather than just a single video struck:

1. Do not create a new account. New accounts created after a suspension are detected and banned. This permanently eliminates your ability to use the platform for commerce.

2. Submit the formal account appeal through the TikTok Business Center if you have a linked business account. Business Center appeals are reviewed separately from creator appeals and typically faster.

3. If you have a TikTok Shop seller account in addition to a creator account, the Shop account and the creator account have separate violation tracking. A creator account violation does not automatically affect the Shop seller account.

4. Be patient. Suspension reviews take 5-15 business days. Do not submit multiple appeals for the same suspension : multiple submissions flag your account as potentially acting in bad faith and can extend the review period.

---

The simplest violation avoidance strategy is to read every caption and text overlay aloud before posting and ask whether each sentence is making a claim you can substantiate. If the answer is no, reframe to describe experience rather than outcome. That one habit eliminates the majority of automated flag triggers.
