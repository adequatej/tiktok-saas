Editing is where most of the difference between a 10K view video and a 10M view video lives. The AI generation step produces the raw material. Editing determines whether that material holds attention all the way through.

The metrics you're optimizing for are completion rate and rewatch rate. Every editing decision in this guide is traceable to one or both of those numbers.

## The Structure of a High-Completion Video

A 30-45 second TikTok Shop video has four distinct zones:

**Zone 1 (0-3s): The hook.** The viewer is deciding whether to keep watching. Nothing from the product should appear here. The frame needs to be visually interesting before any words are spoken or shown.

**Zone 2 (3-15s): The setup.** You establish the problem or desire. The viewer has committed to watching but can still leave. Pacing must be tight. No dead air.

**Zone 3 (15-30s): The reveal.** The product appears and demonstrates its value. This is where the TikTok Shop link placement matters most. Clicks happen here.

**Zone 4 (30-45s): The close.** The CTA and any social proof. Many creators skip this or make it too long. Keep it to 5-7 seconds maximum.

Understanding these zones before you start editing means you're making decisions with intention instead of feel. Every clip goes into a zone. Every zone has a job.

## Clip Pacing

Cut rhythm is the single biggest driver of completion rate that editors get wrong.

The correct pacing for TikTok Shop AI videos is one cut every 2-3 seconds in Zones 2 and 3, and one cut every 3-4 seconds in Zones 1 and 4. Faster than this and the video feels choppy. Slower than this and attention drifts.

How to implement this: import all your clips, drop them on the timeline at full length, then trim from the end of each clip to hit the 2-3 second target. Don't trim the start unless the clip has a slow opening second. Most AI clips have their strongest motion in the first 2-3 seconds : that's what you want on screen.

**Jump cuts vs. cross-dissolves:** Use jump cuts (hard cuts) for the majority of transitions. Cross-dissolves slow pacing and signal to the viewer that the content is less dynamic. Reserve dissolves for Zone 4 transitions only.

## Caption Timing and Style

Captions are not optional. A significant portion of TikTok users watch with sound off, and captions also serve as a second visual track that reinforces the audio for viewers who have sound on.

**Timing:** Captions should appear slightly before the word is spoken, not at the same time. The brain processes text and audio together, and early caption appearance produces a feeling of smoothness. In CapCut, after generating auto-captions, shift the entire caption track 1-2 frames earlier.

**Style settings that work:**
- Font: Bold, high contrast (white text with black outline, or black text with white outline)
- Size: Large enough to read on a phone screen held at arm's length
- Position: Bottom third of frame, not centered (centered captions cover the subject)
- Animation: Fade in, no slide or bounce effects (these feel cheap and distract from the content)

**Keyword emphasis:** In high-performing videos, 2-3 key words per segment are styled differently (larger, different color, or bolded). This is the same principle as highlighting in text : the eye is drawn to the different element. Use this for the product name and the main benefit claim.

## Sound Design

Audio is responsible for a larger share of completion rate than most creators realize. TikTok's recommendation algorithm also considers audio engagement signals.

**The three audio layers:**
1. Voiceover or spoken script
2. Background music
3. Sound effects

All three should be present in every video. Videos with only one or two audio layers feel flat and hold attention less well.

**Background music:** Use TikTok's licensed music library, not external music. Using external music creates copyright flags. Within the library, choose tracks in the "Chill" or "Upbeat" categories that are under 120 BPM. Faster tracks create energy but compete with the voiceover. Volume: set background music to 15-20% of the voiceover volume level.

**Sound effects:** Add a subtle sound effect at every hard cut. This can be an extremely quiet "whoosh" or "click" : the viewer doesn't consciously hear it, but it registers as polish. CapCut has a sound effects library. Use the same sound effect consistently throughout a video for coherence.

**Voiceover volume:** Set to 80-85% maximum volume, not 100%. 100% peaks and distorts on some speakers. 80-85% sounds full without clipping.

## Text Overlays

Text overlays are distinct from captions. Captions transcribe the audio. Text overlays are additional visual information layered on top of the video.

Use text overlays for:
- The hook statement (visible in Zone 1 before audio begins)
- Product callouts ("Link in bio", "TikTok Shop below")
- Key stats or proof points ("10M+ views")
- Price or offer information

**Timing:** The hook text overlay should appear at frame 0 and disappear by second 3. Product callout text should appear when the product first appears in Zone 3 and remain visible through Zone 4.

**Design:** Keep text overlays to one or two words per overlay. Long text overlays are not read : they're skimmed or ignored. If you have more to say, use captions.

## Color Grading

Raw AI footage looks slightly artificial. A color grade that leans warm and slightly desaturated makes AI footage read as more organic and less generated.

In CapCut, use the "Film" preset as a starting point, then:
- Reduce saturation by 10-15%
- Increase warmth by 5-10%
- Add 5-10% vignette

This grade works across most product categories. For tech products, you may want a cooler, more neutral grade : in that case, skip the warmth increase.

## The Final Check Before Export

Before exporting, watch the video in full at 1x speed with the sound off, then again with sound on.

Sound off test: Does the video tell its story visually? Do the text overlays carry the content? If the video is confusing without audio, fix the text and caption layers.

Sound on test: Is the pacing right? Does the cut rhythm feel natural or choppy? Does the voiceover sync with what's on screen?

If both pass, export. If either fails, identify the specific 3-second window where attention would drop and fix only that window. Don't re-edit the whole video for a localized problem.
